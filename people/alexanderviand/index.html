Alexander Viand

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Cambridge Compiler Lab at the University of Cambridge in addition to my work at Belfort Labs. I develop compilers, tools and systems that make privacy-preserving computation more practical and accessible. My research spans fully homomorphic encryption, secure multi-party computation and zero-knowledge proofs, with a focus on abstractions and automation that help non-experts use these technologies. I am also interested in usable security and privacy, and how these technologies interact with society.

I received my PhD from ETH Zürich in May 2023, where I was a doctoral student and research assistant in the Applied Cryptography Group and a member of the Privacy Preserving Systems Lab. I also received my MSc and BSc in Computer Science from ETH Zürich. During my PhD, I visited Tobias Grosser at the University of Edinburgh and Dawn Song at UC Berkeley. Before my current roles, I was a research scientist in the Privacy Technologies Research group at Intel Labs from 2023 to 2025.
